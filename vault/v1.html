The Philosophy

Today, UNCS is one company doing everything — sales, logistics, data, finance, outreach. Every function lives under one roof. That makes it impossible to see what each piece is actually worth, and impossible to scale any one function independently.

The traditional approach would be to try to surgically separate departments from each other — pull logistics away from sales, split finance from operations. But when everything has been woven together for years, those lines are impossible to draw. Every department feels like it is the company. You can't separate what you can't see.

Agent 97 Holdings would take a different approach: start with what's already separate. A call center that doesn't exist yet is obviously its own business — it would have its own P&L, its own revenue model, its own headcount. There's nothing to untangle because it was never tangled in the first place. By building Pixel as a standalone entity from day one, we would create a proof of concept for the entire holding company model. Once Pixel demonstrates how a subsidiary operates independently — billing UNCS for services, maintaining its own books, generating its own metrics — it becomes clear how to apply that same framework to the departments that are intertwined. Pixel would give us the playbook. DIVA, Compass, and Gotham would follow.

Under this model, each subsidiary would operate independently, serve its siblings, and roll up to Agent 97 Holdings. The key: they would be billable to each other. When Pixel reactivates a lead for UNCS, that's an invoice. When DIVA provides data to Pixel, that's a service fee. The holding company would be the only entity that sees the full picture.

How It Works — Step by Step

Step 1 — Validate the Contact

Before anyone picks up the phone, we verify the lead is real and still active.

Identify Role

Many of the 4,095 leads have high-level titles — Presidents, EVPs, executives. We need to know who we're calling.

LinkedIn API Verification

Confirm the contact is still active at the company. LinkedIn activity is the fastest signal — if they're active on the platform, they're still in the seat.

Classify: Customer or Vendor?

Customers and vendors get completely different outreach paths. This split determines everything downstream.

Customer Track — Human First

The call is NOT a product pitch. It's a warm introduction to the DIVA portal. The goal is to get the customer in front of the platform — same experience we built for Menards, but delivered over the phone for 4,000 accounts.

The Call Flow
Open: "Hi, this is [name] from Pixel. I work on behalf of a company that buys and sells products all over the world. We noticed that we haven't done business with you since September 2025, and we're dominating the health & beauty and toy categories right now. I wanted to check in and see how things are going."

If they say "Yes, I've been getting emails from [someone]"
→ Possible data error — may have been reassigned but our records don't reflect it. Flag for review.

If they say "No, haven't heard from you in a while"
→ "That's exactly why I'm calling. Do you have a few minutes right now to look at some products together, or is there a better time I can schedule? I'm happy to coordinate with your assistant if that's easier."

If they have time now:
→ "Great — just open up [customer].diva.com and I'll walk you through it. You can search by category, see what's available, check pricing..."
→ Live walkthrough of the DIVA portal over the phone.

If they want to schedule:
→ Book follow-up. The appointment itself is a win — it's a committed touchpoint.
Why human-first: To get to automation on the customer side, we first need a human interaction. We need to hear what they say, learn their objections, and refine the script. Automation follows data — and we don't have that data yet.
Call card talking points: Developed in collaboration with Chris (data/product context) and Adele (messaging/tone). The pitch isn't "buy this product" — it's "you're missing out on a platform that was built for people like you."

Vendor Track — Automation First

Vendors are simpler. The question is straightforward: "Do you have product available?" The email directs them to the DIVA Product Submission Portal — a tool that already exists and is live in production.

AI-Powered Resource Allocation Tracking

The beauty of AI is that we can actually monitor where resources are being allocated in near real-time — not through timesheets, but through the work itself.

What Gets Tracked

CRM activity (calls logged, leads touched), DIVA queries (dashlet usage, portal configs), email threads tagged to Pixel work, Basecamp tasks, meeting time. Every touchpoint leaves a digital trail.

How It's Measured

AI would classify each employee's activity as UNCS, Pixel, DIVA, or other entity work. Output: a monthly allocation report per person showing actual % split — not an estimate, but a measurement. This is the data that makes inter-entity billing defensible to the IRS.

Why It Matters

When Pixel becomes profitable, it would know exactly what it owes. When DIVA spins out, it would know exactly what UNCS subsidized. This solves the transfer pricing problem — not with guesses, but with data. It also applies to every future subsidiary under Agent 97 Holdings.

Settling the Tab — How UNCS Gets Made Whole

The absorbed cost isn't a gift — it's an investment. Once Pixel (or any subsidiary) reaches profitability, there are three ways to settle up:

ModelHow It WorksWhen to Use
Convertible Loan UNCS's absorbed costs during the POC are logged as a loan from UNCS to Pixel. Once Pixel is profitable, it repays the loan on agreed terms (interest rate, payment schedule). The loan could also convert to equity at a predetermined valuation if both parties prefer. Best for a clean, arms-length relationship. Loan creates a clear obligation. Conversion option gives flexibility.
Equity Buy-In UNCS's absorbed costs are treated as a capital contribution in exchange for equity in Pixel LLC. The value of the contribution determines the ownership split between Agent 97 Holdings (as parent) and UNCS (as investor). Best if UNCS wants ongoing ownership in Pixel rather than cash repayment. Aligns long-term incentives.
Cost-to-Service Transition During the POC, support is absorbed. After the POC, each UNCS employee's time is billed to Pixel at their allocated rate. No retroactive payment — the POC is the investment. Going forward, everything is billable. Simplest. No debt, no equity. The POC is the cost of learning. Post-POC, all entities are on equal footing.
This framework applies to every subsidiary — not just Pixel. When DIVA spins out, the same question arises: how much did UNCS invest in DIVA development, and how does DIVA settle that tab? When Compass launches, same thing. The AI allocation tracking gives us the data to answer these questions for any entity, at any point in time. The model is: absorb during startup, track everything, settle when profitable.

DIVA — The Case for Independence

DIVA is not a feature of UNCS. It's a platform that UNCS happens to use.

Today, DIVA lives inside UNCS — built by Chris Caputo and his team, funded by UNCS, running on UNCS infrastructure. But look at what it actually does: it scans vendor emails, extracts products, matches them to customer profiles, scores opportunities, provides market intelligence, and serves a self-service portal to buyers. That's not a department. That's a product.

What DIVA Does Today

Vendor side: Scans 3.1M+ offers from vendor emails, extracts products from PDFs/spreadsheets, enriches with Keepa pricing and Amazon data, deduplicates, and scores every opportunity 0-100.

Customer side: Matches offers to 51K customer profiles (393K pairings), serves a branded portal where buyers browse categories, see matched products, and check pricing. Already deployed for Menards.

Internal tools: Buyer Prospecting (find contacts at target brands), Repricing Tool (request better pricing from vendors), Chatterbox messaging (desktop, browser, mobile), browser extensions for analyst research.

Why It Should Be Separate

It already serves multiple consumers. UNCS buyers use DIVA daily. Under Agent 97 Holdings, Pixel would use DIVA for call cards and lead scoring. Compass would use it for inventory intelligence. Every subsidiary would need DIVA — but none of them should own it.

It has external value. Any distributor, wholesaler, or B2B marketplace that processes vendor offers could use what DIVA does. The matching engine, the scoring, the portal — none of it is specific to closeout merchandise. It's a horizontal platform trapped inside a vertical business.

It obscures true cost. Right now, DIVA's development, hosting, and AI costs are buried in UNCS overhead. Nobody knows what DIVA actually costs to run — or what it's worth. Separating it would make both numbers visible.

DIVA as a Standalone Entity

Revenue Model

Each Agent 97 Holdings entity would pay DIVA a platform/licensing fee for access. UNCS would pay for buyer portal + matching. Pixel would pay for call card data + lead scoring. Compass would pay for inventory intelligence. External clients would pay SaaS subscription pricing.

Two Portals, Two Markets

Customer-facing: Branded buyer portals (like Menards) where customers browse matched products and pricing.
Vendor-facing: Product Submission Portal where vendors upload inventory via spreadsheet, link, or live connection. These are two distinct products that can be licensed separately.

What Changes

Chris and his team would continue building DIVA — but under DIVA LLC, not as a UNCS project. Development costs, hosting, AI inference — all would become DIVA's P&L. UNCS would become DIVA's first and largest client, not its parent.

Strategic Subsidiaries — Pending CEO Approval

Compass Supply Chain Solutions LLC

UNCS moves millions in freight annually, but logistics lives inside UNCS as a cost center — invisible on the P&L as a business in its own right.

The opportunity: Spin logistics into Compass — a standalone supply chain entity that would serve UNCS as its primary client but could also offer freight, warehousing, and 3PL coordination to external shippers.

Strategic value: Would make UNCS logistics costs transparent. Would create a billable entity that could be valued, grown, or sold independently. Would position Agent 97 Holdings in supply chain — a sector with massive scale potential beyond closeout merchandise.

Gotham Finance LLC

Every UNCS deal requires capital — purchase order funding, deal financing, credit extension. Today that's handled internally with no visibility into the true cost of capital per deal.

The opportunity: Gotham Finance would become the capital arm of Agent 97 Holdings — financing deals for UNCS (and eventually external buyers) as a standalone financial services entity.

Revenue model: Gotham would earn a financing fee or interest spread on every deal it funds. UNCS would get faster deal execution. Gotham would get a portfolio of short-duration, asset-backed receivables.

Strategic value: Would separate trading risk from financing risk. Would create a financial entity with its own balance sheet. Would open the door to factoring, PO financing for external clients, and eventually a lending operation that isn't tied to UNCS deal flow.
