### Question 2: What is your experience as a group facilitator, not in training groups on content? Please share whether you are formally trained. If not, what experience led you to become a facilitator.

I am not formally trained as a facilitator through a certification program — that is what this application represents for me: formalizing two decades of practice into recognized expertise.

My facilitation experience is rooted in Forum itself. As Spouse Forum Officer for YPO Miami, I designed and facilitated the chapter's first professional spouse forum. This was not inheriting an existing group — it was building from zero. I recruited members, facilitated the initial sessions, established group norms and confidentiality agreements, led structured exercises including modified life-walks and presentation formats, and developed the incoming moderator. Over the course of that work, the group moved from a collection of strangers to a Forum that held real trust and vulnerability.

I then took what I learned and seeded a Forum for Business program inside my own organization using the YPO International framework. This required adapting the Forum model for a corporate leadership team — people who report to me, who had no prior exposure to Forum, and who needed a different kind of safety to engage honestly. I facilitated those sessions directly, designing exercises that addressed the specific dynamics of a leadership team inside an operating business.

What led me to facilitation was feedback. Forum members pointed out that the way I hold space — reading the room, asking the question that unlocks the real conversation, staying calm in tension — was a skill worth developing. I hadn't thought of it as facilitation until people I trusted told me it was. That feedback, combined with my experience building forums from the ground up, is what led me here.
